Method

Newshound follows a scent. Prediction markets on Polymarket and Kalshi are where people put money on what happens next, and when expectations about something shift sharply, it usually means something happened. The shift is the scent. It is not the story.

Every hour the hound checks about 55,000 markets against an hour earlier. Sharp moves with real money behind them are grouped by what they are about, and each group is scored by how much it shifted expectations. Anything that scores high enough gets chased.

The chase

Claude works out what kind of development would produce the shift, then goes looking: news reports, official statements and press releases, court dockets and filings, government sites, posts from the people involved. It reads what it finds, checks when each piece was published, and writes up what happened.

Only what it catches is published. A story needs a source that came back from a real search, reported the development, and was published before expectations moved (or within a few minutes of it). Code checks this, not the writer. Wire services, established outlets and primary documents can explain a story. Aggregators, re-posters and partisan blogs can appear as context but never count as the report that explains it.

On the trail

When the hunt finds nothing, the lead goes on the trail as an open question, and the hound checks again after 1, 6 and 24 hours. If reporting turns up later, the lead becomes a story, and the page notes how long after the shift the first report came.

How shifts are scored

For each market, the points it moved are multiplied by the dollars traded during the move, and weighted up when trading ran far above its usual pace. A story's score adds this up across its markets on a log scale from 0 to 100, so every 10 points means ten times as much. Stories scoring 25 or more are chased; smaller ones are noted as things the hound is watching.

  • Extraordinary shift: 50 and up
  • Major shift: 38 and up
  • Large shift: 34 and up
  • Notable shift: 30 and up
  • Moderate shift: 25 and up

What it misses

The page reflects what people bet on. Politics, sports and economics carry the most money, and news with no market behind it never gives off a scent. Live games, weather, crypto prices and other markets that just track a public feed are ignored.
