Saudi Arabia says East-West oil pipeline was attacked near Medina

November 10, 2026, updated 6:10 PM ET

An official at Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry told the state Saudi Press Agency on Monday, November 9, that the East-West crude pipeline had been targeted in the Medina region. The attack caused a limited fire, which emergency crews put out. The official said no one was hurt and pipeline operations were not affected. The statement did not say who carried out the attack. Yemen's Houthis have repeatedly hit Saudi oil sites with drones and missiles, and early on Tuesday the Saudi-led coalition said it had shot down two missiles fired at Riyadh and a third aimed at the south of the country. The coalition blamed the Houthis for those launches.

Why it matters

With flows through the Strait of Hormuz disrupted, Saudi Arabia has been sending about 4 million barrels of crude a day through this pipeline to the Red Sea port of Yanbu, roughly 4 percent of world supply. Monday's attack is the latest after drone strikes in September shut the line down for days, which shows the kingdom's main way around Hormuz is still open to attack.
