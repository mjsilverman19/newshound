Trump calls Iran war "effectively over" but keeps naval blockade in place

November 10, 2026, updated 6:10 PM ET

President Donald Trump met Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani at the White House on November 9. At an unscheduled briefing afterward, he said the war with Iran was "effectively over" militarily. He added that the US would keep "the status quo" at the request of Gulf states, and he said that meant continuing the US naval blockade of Iranian ports and the newly announced Task Force Iron Gate, which targets land and Caspian Sea trade. He said new military action looked "quite unlikely" but that US forces were "locked and loaded." Earlier, Secretary of State Marco Rubio had called some of Iran's conditions "outrageous," and US and Gulf officials had played down hopes of an imminent deal. Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei dismissed talk of negotiations as "fake news." On November 10, Qatar said there was progress but that it could not say how close an agreement was.

Why it matters

Trump has signaled that the blockade will continue and that new US strikes are unlikely. That points to a long standoff with neither war nor peace, with no deal in sight, and leaves the Strait of Hormuz and Iranian trade under pressure.
