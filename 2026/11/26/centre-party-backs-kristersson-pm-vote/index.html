Swedish Centre Party leaders back Kristersson as prime minister ahead of Monday vote

November 26, 2026, updated 6:11 PM ET

On Thursday, November 26, Centre Party leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist said a majority of the party board and parliamentary group had voted to recommend backing caretaker Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson in the Riksdag's vote on Monday, November 30. She said the decision was not unanimous. The plan would bring the Centre Party into a minority government with the Moderates, Christian Democrats and Liberals. It follows the collapse of talks on a centre-left government after the Left Party turned down an offer from the Social Democrats, Centre and Greens last weekend. The party's council (förtroenderådet), its highest decision-making body between congresses, meets on Sunday to make the final call, and some members have openly criticised the leadership's recommendation.

Why it matters

The Centre Party campaigned for a centre-left government under Magdalena Andersson, so this switch could give Kristersson a way back to power after the right-wing bloc lost the September election. Monday's vote is the third of a maximum of four; if all four fail, Sweden must hold a snap election within three months.
