Iran says more tankers hit mines in Hormuz as talks on reopening the strait stall

November 5, 2026, updated 11:22 PM ET

Early on November 5, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two oil tankers and a tugboat caught fire after trying to use unauthorised routes through the Strait of Hormuz. Later that day Iran's army said three more tankers struck naval mines east of the strait. That brought Iran's count to at least five damaged tankers in the day, following its November 4 claim that four tankers were destroyed by mines. Britain's UKMTO also reported a tanker hit by two projectiles north of Qatar late on November 4. All of this came a day after Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, called Washington's reply to Tehran's seven conditions for reopening the strait unconvincing. Oman said on November 5 that it was still working with Iran on a joint system to control traffic, and Qatar is still mediating, but neither side has announced a deal. No single new diplomatic setback stands out. The shift reflects a steady run of Iranian claims of attacks on shipping while the talks go nowhere.

Why it matters

By its own account, Iran is still enforcing its closure of the strait by force. Only a handful of ships cross each day, against about 20 before the war. A quick US-Iran or Iran-Oman arrangement to reopen this major oil route now looks further off, even as Qatar and Oman keep talking. The incidents are Iranian claims and have not been independently verified.
