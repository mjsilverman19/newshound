Strong October jobs report revives case for a December Fed rate hike

November 5, 2026, updated 9:24 AM ET

The October employment report came out on Friday, November 6. It showed US payrolls rose by 165,000, the unemployment rate dropped to 4.1%, and earlier months were revised up. Economists quoted by Morningstar said the strong hiring makes it more likely the Federal Reserve raises rates at its December 8-9 meeting. In the same days, New York Fed President John Williams said inflation is too high, San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said the economy's strength leaves room to fight inflation, and Beth Hammack and Thomas Barkin spoke in favor of raising rates gradually. A day earlier, on Thursday, November 5, the Nasdaq's sharpest one-day drop in 19 months had led investors to step back from expecting a hike. That drop followed the November 3 election and fears of new limits on building AI data centers.

Why it matters

The Fed held its policy rate at 3.75% to 4% on October 28 and has said incoming data will decide its next step. A solid labor market combined with inflation officials call too high makes a quarter-point increase in December more likely. That would raise borrowing costs for households and businesses heading into 2027.
