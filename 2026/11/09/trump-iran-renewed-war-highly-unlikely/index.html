Trump says renewed war with Iran is "highly unlikely" if talks make progress

November 9, 2026, updated 6:12 PM ET

President Donald Trump said on Sunday, November 8, that it is "highly unlikely" he will need to send the US back to war with Iran as long as talks on Tehran's nuclear program and reopening the Strait of Hormuz make progress. He repeated the phrase five times in a 20-minute phone interview with CNN's Jim Sciutto. He also warned that three US aircraft carriers in the region are ready and that US forces could knock down 38 Iranian bridges within an hour. The same day, US Central Command expanded pressure on Iran with a new "Iron Gate" task force aimed at cutting Iran's land supply routes, adding to a naval blockade of Iranian ports that has run for more than 100 days. A day later, after meeting Qatar's prime minister, Trump said renewed military action was "quite unlikely."

Why it matters

Trump's comments indicate that Washington, for now, plans to keep squeezing Iran economically through the naval and land blockades instead of returning to large-scale military operations. That shifts attention to Qatar-led diplomacy. Oil prices fell when markets opened after the interview.
