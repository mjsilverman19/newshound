KMT leads Taiwan local elections with at least 10 of 22 mayor and magistrate seats

November 28, 2026, updated 9:11 AM ET

At Taiwan's nationwide local elections on Saturday, Nov. 28, the opposition Kuomintang had claimed 10 of the 22 mayor and county magistrate races as of 9:18 p.m. local time, according to the Focus Taiwan/CNA live tally. The ruling DPP had nine and an independent had one. KMT candidates also declared victory in the two undecided races. In Yunlin, Alice Chang (Chang Chia-chun) claimed a margin of about 863 votes over the DPP's Liu Chien-kuo. In Hualien, Yu Shu-chen claimed a win over independent Chang Chun, who said he would ask for a recount. Both races are within the 0.3 percent margin that allows a recount. Among the big cities, the KMT kept Taipei (Chiang Wan-an), New Taipei (Lee Shu-chuan) and Taoyuan (Chang San-cheng). The DPP kept Tainan and Kaohsiung and took Taichung, where KMT legislator Johnny Chiang conceded to Ho Hsin-chun. The DPP also flipped Hsinchu County, Changhua, Yilan and Chiayi City, while the KMT took Penghu back. In a referendum held the same day, voters approved abolishing the "nuclear-free homeland" policy, with more than 7 million yes votes. All results stay unofficial until the Central Election Commission confirms them.

Why it matters

The KMT goes into the 2028 presidential race still holding the most local governments, including the capital region. But the DPP took back Taichung and several pan-blue counties, so the KMT ends up with fewer seats than the 14 it won in 2022. Separately, the referendum result puts pressure on the government to reverse its nuclear phase-out policy.
